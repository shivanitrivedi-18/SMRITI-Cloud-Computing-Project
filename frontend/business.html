SMRITI — Business Model

AI-assisted memory-support platform for families of older adults · Pitch Fest 2026 · Software product

1 · Production / Operations

Product & uniqueness

  • Personal Memory Vault: family saves photos, voice notes, people & places.
  • Connected memories: link people ↔ events ↔ places into a memory network.
  • 3 cognitive activities: Memory Garden, Focus & Find, My Daily Story.
  • Adaptive difficulty + trend forecast & early check-in flag against the person's own baseline.
  • Caregiver dashboard, reminders, mood check-ins, voice guidance, private family profiles.

Why it is different

  • Generic brain-games are not personalised; SMRITI uses the person's own life memories.
  • Built for the family caregiver, not only the patient.
  • Designed for Indian families (full Hindi interface is on the roadmap; simple WhatsApp-style flow, low price).

Production process (software)

  • Agile 2-week sprints: design → build → test → release.
  • Stack: HTML/CSS/JS + FastAPI (Python) + SQLite → PostgreSQL at scale.
  • Statistical engine (EWMA + least-squares trend) in pure Python — no costly GPU.
  • Continuous backups; staged releases; user feedback loop.

Resources required ("raw material")

  • Cloud VPS (2 vCPU / 4 GB): ₹1,500–3,000 / month
  • Object storage for photos/audio: ~₹1 per user / month
  • Domain + SSL: ~₹800 / year
  • Payment gateway: ~2% per transaction
  • Founder laptop (already owned)

Capacity planning

  • One VPS comfortably serves ~2,000 family profiles (planning estimate; load-test before launch).
  • Scale step: managed PostgreSQL + object storage at ~1,500 users (+₹4,000/month).
  • Support capacity: 1 part-time executive per ~300 paying families.

Quality control & "inventory"

  • Automated syntax/unit checks before every release; private-profile access tests.
  • Clinician/psychologist advisor reviews activity content and wording.
  • Digital inventory: activity library, memory media (size-limited), daily encrypted backups.
  • Privacy by design: hashed passwords, per-profile access checks, no real data in demo.

2 · Marketing Management

Market need

An estimated 5.3 million people over 60 live with dementia in India (ARDSI, Dementia in India 2020), and each has family caregivers who face heavy emotional and physical strain. India's 60+ population is projected to reach ~19% by 2050.

Target customers

  • Primary: adult children (35–60) of seniors with memory concerns — urban & tier-2 cities.
  • Secondary: old-age homes, day-care centres, memory clinics, NGOs.
  • Influencers: neurologists, geriatricians, psychologists.

Segmentation

  • Early memory concerns (prevention & engagement)
  • Diagnosed mild stages (structured routine)
  • Institutions (many profiles, one dashboard)

Pricing strategy

  • Free: 1 profile, basic activities — builds trust.
  • Family ₹199/month (₹1,999/yr): up to 3 profiles, memory media, forecast, reports.
  • Care Centre ₹1,499/month: up to 25 profiles, care-centre overview of every resident.
  • Value anchor: less than one doctor-visit snack-bill per month.

Promotion

  • Hindi/English YouTube & Instagram reels: "5 memory games for your parents".
  • WhatsApp & Facebook caregiver communities.
  • Free awareness sessions with ARDSI-type NGOs and old-age homes.
  • Doctor referral cards; Alzheimer's Awareness Month campaigns.

Sales & distribution

  • Web app / installable PWA — no app-store dependency.
  • Free tier → in-app upgrade (UPI / cards via payment gateway).
  • Direct B2B demos to care homes & clinics.

3 · Human Resource Management

Organisation

Lean startup: Founder-CEO/CTO leads product & engineering; a part-time Care Success Executive handles families; a clinical advisor guides content. Roles are added as revenue grows.

Recruitment & training

  • Recruit through college placement cells and internships.
  • 2-week onboarding on empathy, privacy and the product.
  • Monthly review of support tickets and feedback.

5 · Question & Answer

Is SMRITI a medical device / does it diagnose dementia?No. It is an assistive memory-support and activity tool. It never diagnoses; its trend flag only compares a person's recent activity with their own baseline and suggests a check-in or a doctor visit.
Where is the AI?Adaptive difficulty and a statistical trend engine (composite score, exponential moving average, least-squares trend and 3-session projection). It is rule-based and statistical, not a trained deep-learning model — which keeps it explainable, cheap and private. Roadmap: language-model reminiscence companion once clinically reviewed.
How do you make money and what are your margins?Family subscription ₹199/month with ~₹24 variable cost (88% contribution margin) and a Care-Centre plan at ₹1,499/month. Break-even is about 172 paying families.
How did you get ₹1.5 lakh and when do you recover it?Founder savings / small grant or incubator support. At the base case (profit ₹40,000/month at month 12) cumulative cash turns positive in about 14 months — see the calculator.
Why would families pay when free games exist?Free games are generic. SMRITI uses the family's own photos, voices and stories, gives the caregiver a dashboard, and flags changes early — the reason families pay is connection plus peace of mind.
How do you protect sensitive data?Password hashing, private per-profile access checks, authenticated media, size-limited uploads. Before real launch: HTTPS-only cookies, encrypted backups, security review and a privacy policy compliant with India's DPDP Act. The demo uses fictional data only.
Who are your competitors and what is your edge?Generic brain-training apps, scrapbooks and clinic therapy. Edge: personal memories + caregiver dashboard + Indian-language focus + low price.
What if raw material (cloud) costs rise?Cloud and storage are about 10% of revenue at base case; costs scale with users, and annual plans plus a small price rise absorb increases.
How will you acquire the first 100 customers?NGO and old-age-home pilots, doctor referrals, caregiver WhatsApp communities and Hindi video content; free tier converts a share to paid.
What is the biggest risk?Trust and low digital literacy of seniors. Mitigation: simple large-button elder mode, voice guidance, family-led setup, and a support executive.

Source for prevalence: ARDSI, "Dementia in India 2020" report. All financial figures are planning estimates.
